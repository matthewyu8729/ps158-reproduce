THE MODEL, EXPLAINED

One chain.
Four simple equations.

Lower labour requirements can reduce costs. Lower prices can increase purchases. The balance determines the human hours needed.

Every input describes a cumulative change by 2035. These are scenarios, not annual growth rates or forecasts.

01

Human hours needed per unit

r = (1 − z)[(1 − a) + a / (1 + g)]

g is the output-per-hour gain, a is the assisted share of remaining work, and z is a separate fully automated output share. A 25% productivity gain means 20% less time at full adoption.

02

Net production-cost saving

c = s(1 − r) − k

s is the affected labour's share of original total cost. k is new AI cost as a fraction of that original cost. Wages and other unit costs stay constant.

03

Prices and purchases

p = 1 − τc   ·   q = p−ε

τ is price pass-through. ε is demand elasticity: at ε = 2, a 1% price fall leads to approximately 2% more purchases for a small price change. p and q are ratios to the same-year counterfactual.

04

Total human hours

R = r × q

R > 1 with r < 1 means backfire: demand expands enough to more than offset time savings. R < 1 means hours fall. More output alone does not prove a labour rebound.

READ THE RESULTS WITH CONTEXT

A laboratory for assumptions.
A starting point for research.

The model estimates direct human hours demanded at fixed wages. It does not estimate unemployment, wages, welfare, or economy-wide employment.

What comes from evidence?

Software study ↗ · The 26.08% anchor measures weekly completed tasks, not directly hourly productivity.

Customer-support study ↗ · The 15% anchor measures issues resolved per hour in one firm's operation.

Graphic design: the 30% anchor is assumed. All preset adoption, cost, elasticity, pass-through and automation settings are illustrative.

Define the market: commissioned software differs from licences. Outsourced support differs from support bundled into another product; embedded support may need a host-product demand model.

Background growth and US work

In the additional assumptions, B changes non-AI output demand and d changes the relative amount of work retained in the US. Then H₂₀₃₅/H₂₀₂₅ = B × d × R. Rising hours due to B alone are not an AI-induced rebound. The main headline isolates R, holding these factors fixed. These multipliers are separate from the price-demand mechanism.
